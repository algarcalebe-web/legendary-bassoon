Shortlist from `phase3.md`:

- **S2** opposite-season Brazil-Italy home exchange (#13);

### 4.6 Network-effects test (S2, the only candidate with network effects)

| Winner-take-all condition | Holds? |
|---|---|
| 1 High multi-homing costs | **No.** Owners can list on ThirdHome and HomeExchange at the same time |
| 2 Strong positive network effects | Yes, cross-side (Brazilian owners want Italian owners and vice versa); negative congestion only at peak weeks |
| 3 Limited demand for differentiated features | Partly. Luxury owners want specific homes and dates |

**Result:** condition 1 fails, so scale alone won't lock the market. Platform playbook if pursued:
- win marquee owners first (named palazzi; landmark Jurere/Santa Catarina homes);
- subsidise the scarcer side (likely Italian palazzo owners) with free membership;
- beware envelopment by ThirdHome adding a "Brazil-Italy" collection.

### 5.5 Trade-Off Method interview guide (indifference questions only; no direct WTP questions)

Each question pins the respondent's switching point between their next best alternative and our offer. Run in person with a short explanation; record the value of X.

| # | Hypothesis | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | S1 | "Option A: you organise your own viewing trip to Rome and use a local agent. Option B: we host your trip, including two off-market palazzo viewings, and you pay the trip cost, credited against the commission if you buy. At what trip cost for B would A and B be equally attractive to you?" |
| 2 | S1 | "Option A: a hosted trip that costs you nothing up front. Option B: the same trip at a cost of EUR X, with written independence from any sale. At what X are you indifferent?" (prices the conflict of interest) |
| 3 | S1 | "Option A: viewings only. Option B: viewings plus a curated leisure programme (access, dining, transfers). How many extra days of your own planning would make A as good as B?" (value of time) |
| 4 | S2 | "Option A: rent a comparable home in Italy for your summer week at your usual cost. Option B: swap your Santa Catarina home for a vetted palazzo for that week. At what annual membership fee would you be indifferent between A and B?" |
| 5 | S2 | "Option A: a global exchange club with thousands of homes. Option B: a corridor club with 20 personally vetted homes in Italy. How many vetted homes would B need for you to be indifferent?" |
| 6 | S3 | "Option A: commit your jet-card or club deposit after your own checks. Option B: commit after an independent solvency report. At what report price would A and B be equally attractive, for a deposit of US$X (their actual amount)?" |
| 7 | S4 | "Option A: a planning fee of EUR 300, non-refundable. Option B: a planning fee of EUR X, fully refunded if agreed outcomes are missed. At what X are you indifferent?" |
| 8 | S4 | "Option A: a villa with no swap commitment. Option B: the same villa with a guaranteed move to an equal-or-better home if it doesn't match on arrival. At what nightly premium are you indifferent?" |

## Bottom line for the write-up
- **No option has a sustainable advantage today.**
- **S1 has the widest WTP-cost wedge.** The capture comes from the commission, the edge from relationships, and it fits the founders' time budget. Its open risks are scope (it is leisure travel as a funnel to a property sale) and whether the brokerage permits it.
- **S2 and S3 are cheap side tests.**
- **S4 is a feature, not a business.**
- **C stays a benchmark.** Its Santa Catarina leg should be disinvested unless the waitlist test passes.
