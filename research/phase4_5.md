### 4.6 Network-effects test (A is the only candidate with network effects)

| Winner-take-all condition | Holds for A? |
|---|---|
| 1 High multi-homing costs | **No.** Members also use Indagare, Reddit, advisors |
| 2 Strong positive network effects | Yes, same-side (more trusted reviewers). Negative congestion: exclusivity falls as it grows |
| 3 Limited demand for differentiated features | No. Tastes and destinations differ widely |

**Result:** no lock-in from scale. Platform playbook if pursued:
- win marquee members first;
- keep it free or subsidised for contributors;
- charge consumers of recommendations;
- beware envelopment by Indagare or AmEx adding a verified tier.

### 5.3 Monetization mechanisms

| Mechanism | Application |
|---|---|
| Third-degree price discrimination | A: individual vs family tiers, fenced by access depth. B: individual report vs family-office subscription, fenced by coverage and updates |
| Two-part pricing | A: membership + paid introductions. B: subscription + per report. D: retainer + per trip |
| Bundling vs unbundling | **Unbundle by default.** Bundle F+E only if relative WTP reverses across segments (DIY planners value the audit; delegators value the guarantee). Test it |
| Peak-load pricing | D, E: higher fees for peak-season trips, when failures and capacity limits concentrate |

### 5.4 Value-added services

| Service | Rivals | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Wealth verification (A) | Nobody found | Value-price inside membership |
| Solvency report (B) | Nobody vets financials | Value-price |
| Pay-per-trip desk (D) | Rivals charge retainers | Charge near rivals' per-trip equivalent |
| Outcome guarantee (E) | Nobody found | Value-price inside the fee |
| Itinerary audit (F) | Rivals charge US$99-200 (cost-plus) | Charge the rival's price at entry; differentiate on scope |
| Messaging concierge | Advisors give it free | Give free; discount for clients who use it less |

**What customers could offer in exchange for perks:**
- verified reviews and trip reports (A);
- provider contracts and disclosures (B's database);
- referrals of peers and family offices;
- early booking and flexible dates (E's swap capacity);
- permission to use anonymised failure data (F).

### 5.5 Trade-Off Method interview guide (indifference questions only; no direct WTP questions)

| # | Option | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | "Option A: plan using reviews, AI and friends at no fee. Option B: recommendations only from wealth-verified travellers like you, for an annual fee of X. At what X are A and B equally attractive?" |
| 2 | A | "Option A: Indagare-type membership at its current price. Option B: a verified-peer network with fewer reviews. How many verified reviews of your next destination would B need for you to be indifferent?" |
| 3 | B | "Option A: commit your deposit of US$[their amount] after your own checks. Option B: commit after an independent solvency report costing X. At what X are you indifferent?" |
| 4 | D | "Option A: your current assistant or desk handles travel. Option B: an external desk paid only per trip, at X per trip. At what X are you indifferent?" |
| 5 | D | "Option A: a dedicated retainer desk. Option B: pay-per-trip with a 4-hour response time. What response time would make B as good as A?" |
| 6 | E | "Option A: a non-refundable planning fee of US$350. Option B: a fee of X, fully refunded if agreed outcomes are missed. At what X are you indifferent?" |
| 7 | E | "Option A: a villa with no swap commitment. Option B: the same villa with a guaranteed move to equal-or-better if it doesn't match on arrival. At what nightly premium are you indifferent?" |
| 8 | F | "Option A: you check your AI-made itinerary yourself. Option B: an expert audit of closures, transfers, border timing and insurance gaps, at X. At what X are you indifferent?" |

Ask questions 2 and 5 of people outside the target segment too (Deneffe: qualitative interviews include non-customers).

## Bottom line
- **No option has a sustainable advantage today, and none scores above 14/20 on outside-in criteria.**
- **A (verified peer network) is the strongest outside-in.** It is the only option aimed at the top-3 pains with a mechanism that could become a moat. It is contestable by Indagare, and network effects won't lock the market.
- **B has the largest gap** (nobody vets provider finances), but the pain is rare and willingness to pay is unproven.
- **D has the best willingness-to-pay evidence**, but incumbents are entrenched.
- **E + F work as a linked test.** F is a declining market because AI improves.
- **C ranks below all five** because operators already meet its needs (score 9).
- **Inside-out note (facts only).** The founders' competences fit B best and fail D's time requirement. This is reported, not used in the ranking.

## Revision (UHNW scope): Phases 4-5 applied to the final shortlist

**This revision supersedes the A-F shortlist above.** The final shortlist (`phase3_uhnw.md`) is:
- **counterparty assurance for private travel**, the lead: a solvency-and-legitimacy standard, then insured deposit protection;
- the verified peer network;
- the insurer-underwritten outcome guarantee.

The full Deneffe treatment lives in the deck (`deck/Private_Travel_Assurance_Strategy_Deck.pptx`, slides 15-27). In summary:

- **How to win.**
  - Minimum requirements: ratings that withstand legal challenge; independence; confidentiality; insurer-backed claims; a published method.
  - Primary route: meet an important unmet need better. Secondary: replace part of the broker's 10-20% vouching markup.
  - The wedge: buyers' willingness to pay is anchored on the deposit at risk; marginal cost is analyst time, and claims sit with the insurer.
- **Six forces across the shortlist.** Outlook: assurance medium-high; peer network medium (strongest substitutes); guarantee low on its own.
- **Funnel, rivals, advantage, competences, Strategic Position Analysis:**
  - The lead is offensive (first-mover standard, insurer partner); the peer network is opportunistic.
  - The guarantee folds into the lead; movement privacy becomes a module.
  - The Rome-Santa Catarina curator: disinvest.
- **Pricing:**
  - Two-part pricing on both sides; third-degree price discrimination fenced by coverage depth.
  - Share of premium on protection; bundling only at a partner's checkout.
  - Peak-load pricing in peak seasons (hypothesis).
  - Full value-added-services rule plus the two-way menu.
  - 8 indifference questions (Trade-Off Method).
- **Operational strategy:** six zero-capital tests with pass marks; go/no-go at day 90.
