# Family mobility office: staff-mobility wedge, final strategy analysis

Date: 2026-10-06. This merges six draft modules and four adversarial critiques. Evidence grades: H = primary document or live page read; M = named publisher via a search summary; L = a single ad, snippet or vendor claim. CALC = our arithmetic, shown. ASSUMPTION = our input, with no source. NOT FOUND = searched for in the research files, not found. All scores are our assessment.

## 1. Verdict

UHNW families with several homes need trusted household staff who travel and rotate with them lawfully, and the problem is real. In one agency's database, only 2.36% of childcare candidates are available for rota work and 4.82% travel regularly (M&M-BTB; H for that database, M as market evidence). The UK domestic-worker visa lasts 6 months and needs 12 months' prior work for the same employer (GOV.UK 2026, H). The UAE fines illegal employment AED 50-200k (Decree-Law 9/2022, M-H). Families already pay agency fees of 15-25% (M), travel premiums of 25-40% to the staff (M&M-web, H) and GBP 300-400 a day for London temps (one ad, L). Evidence that they would pay an intermediary a recurring fee is weak. No retainers were found, household payroll costs GBP 276-474 a year (Nannytax, H), and the closest rival, Morgan & Mallet (M&M), already sells multi-country employer-of-record (EOR) services for household staff. Three things the model needs are NOT FOUND: the segment's size, a UK or multi-country EOR other than M&M that will employ domestic workers (TEAM does so in the US; its international offer is NOT FOUND), and lawful routes for accompanying staff in Schengen countries, Switzerland and Monaco. So: do not build yet. Run the 90-day validation (section 9), and fund a pilot only from client payments, after the counsel, EOR, insurer and Trade-Off Method (TOM) gates pass. The WTP-cost wedge decides it. On every measurable cost we lose to the family's next best alternative (NBA). An employed reliever costs GBP 475-479 per working day before idle days (CALC: (99,000 + 5,529-6,452) ÷ 220), against GBP 300-400 for a temp. A partner EOR costs GBP 5.5-6.5k a year, against GBP 0.4-0.6k for household payroll. The measurable wedge is therefore minus GBP 7.6-13.9k per position-year (CALC, 5.3). For EBIT to reach zero at 15 positions, families must value guaranteed, lawful cover at **GBP 13.6-19.9k per position-year** (about US$17.7-25.8k; economic profit needs about GBP 0.2k more, CALC 5.5), net of the control they give up and the risk of a new entrant, as shown by TOM switching points. That is about what they already pay staff to travel (GBP 16.5-23.6k, CALC 5.1). Even so, the pilot year loses money at any fee below about GBP 36k, and profit must come before M&M, whose wedge is wider, copies the model.

### 3.4 Minimum requirements

(1) A written lawful route per leg (counsel advises, we coordinate). (2) A legal employer: an EOR that takes domestic workers, or our own entity. (3) Liability and trip medical cover bound before travel. (4) Agency-standard vetting and NDAs (77% of LA PA placements need NDAs, M&M-BTB). (5) A replacement guarantee on agency terms; agencies' contingent fees already come with replacement guarantees (Fees, M), so it is a minimum, not a differentiator. (6) Steady, benefited staff contracts. (7) Welfare safeguards: own passports, logged days off, a hotline. (8) A vouching channel or references.

### 5.2 Method choice; cost-plus rejected

| Benefit or drawback | Method |
|---|---|
| Relief and payroll avoided; PA hours (counted in interviews, priced at pay) | Value map |
| Penalty avoided | Only when the family's NBA is non-compliant |
| Guarantee, continuity, lawful status (positives); control loss, unfamiliar relief staff, entrant risk, privacy, non-Western passports (negatives) | TOM |

Direct methods (Van Westendorp, Gabor-Granger, open-ended, monadic, key-purchasing-criteria ranking) bias results and over-predict purchase; econometrics needs history a new offer lacks. Cost-plus is rejected: the bench is fixed per reliever, so low volume raises unit cost, price rises and volume falls again (a death spiral). Price is set where MR = MC on the TOM demand curve; fixed costs decide only whether economic profit is positive.

### 5.5 Economic profit

EVA = NOPAT − WACC × IC, with fixed costs inside NOPAT. IC = payroll float + pre-launch counsel and insurance (about GBP 23k, costing GBP 2.8k a year at 12%) + any notice-gap reserve + entity capital (NOT FOUND).

| Case | Positions | Break-even fee per position-year | Required V | EBIT at a GBP 25k fee |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot (1 reliever, N = 6) | 6 | 35,957-36,569 | about 19-25k | **−65.7k to −69.4k** |
| Year 3 (3 relievers, N = 5) | 15 | 30,628-31,362 | **13,628-19,862** | −84.4k to −95.4k |

How the table is built (CALC):
- Break-even fee = floor + 90,000 ÷ positions.
- Required V = break-even fee − measurable ceiling.
- Year 3 at a GBP 35k fee: EBIT = 15 × (35,000 − 25,362) − 90,000 = GBP 54.6k, so NOPAT = GBP 40.9k and EVA ≈ GBP 38k. That needs V of GBP 18-23.5k.
- If we keep 50% of the wedge W, break-even takes 36 positions at W = 5k, 18 at 10k and 9 at 20k.

**Notice gap (Model A).** On a 6-month staff contract (ASSUMPTION; rota placements usually last 6-12 months, M&M-BTB), a client who leaves after month 1 on 30 days' notice strands up to 5 months × GBP 8,250 of loaded pay (99,000 ÷ 12) = **GBP 41,250 per worker** (CALC). A 12-month contract would strand up to 11 months.

**Logic.** Billing in advance cuts IC to the pre-launch spend but does not change the sign. The table is EBIT break-even (the float's capital charge is already in the floor); covering the GBP 2.8k charge on pre-launch spend adds about GBP 0.2k per position (CALC: 2,760 ÷ 15 = 184). So EP is positive only if V exceeds about GBP 13.8-20.0k across 15 positions (GBP 13.6-19.9k for EBIT), inside the lead window.

### 5.6 Monetization choices

| Mechanism | Decision |
|---|---|
| Value metric | **Membership per position-year**: value and cost scale with positions, and it pre-funds the bench. Relief days at incremental cost only (flights, per diem, or a temp-desk day when the bench is full). Fee per rotation with half on completion dropped, since it creates receivables. Outcome credit deferred: no insurer backing |
| Discrimination and fences | The market is opaque: fees unpublished, no retainers, infrequent purchases. Use **third-degree pricing with published tiers**. Discounts only from the two-way menu, since negotiation rewards skill, invites discount creep and leaks through peers and channels. Fences: response time (7 days, 72 h, 24 h), dedicated or shared reliever, cleared jurisdictions, NDA-cleared staff, and a per-household licence against MFO arbitrage |
| Two-part pricing | The fixed membership captures surplus and prepays the bench; the usage fee equals incremental cost. Planned relief sits inside the membership, because WTP per day falls with use |
| Bundling reversal test | TOM switching values per segment, not rankings, on four separable items: (i) relief club; (ii) compliance coordination; (iii) full employer pool; (iv) record. Hypothesis: SFOs with existing staff value (ii) above (i); principals value (i) above (ii); S4 values (ii) far above (i). If the order reverses, use mixed bundling; otherwise sell a base plus options |
| Peak-load pricing | Hypothesis: no peak prices or volumes in the files. Justified by capacity (one reliever, one household at a time). If T1 calendars show coinciding peaks, N falls: charge a peak premium, give an off-peak credit |
| Elasticity | Plot the TOM share switching at each fee, find the revenue peak, launch where MR = MC. Switching points clustered just above break-even mean demand is elastic there |

## 7. Trade-Off Method interview guide

**Protocol:** easy preference opener; record the respondent's best alternative and its actual cost; the respondent states X (never anchor); find the switching point; iterate by halving or doubling to catch non-linearity; one combined question tests additivity; face to face or assisted video. Non-monetary X is converted with the respondent's own anchor (e.g. temp days × their day rate for one uncovered week).

**Baseline facts (not WTP questions):** relief days bought last year and cost; travelling staff; who decides; calendar and peaks; payroll and counsel spend.

| # | Respondent | A (their best alternative) vs B (offer) | X they state |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Family (opener) | Your nanny with today's uncovered weeks vs your nanny plus a named second carer you have met | A or B first; then the annual amount added to B that makes them equal |
| 2 | Family | You pick each relief person from a shortlist vs we assign from a vetted pool, after an introduction | Fee reduction in B (control loss) |
| 3 | Family | Best-effort temp desk vs cover guaranteed within 72 h | Fee difference; iterate at 24 h and 7 days |
| 4 | Family | You employ and hire counsel per trip vs our partner employs on every leg | Fee difference (Model A) |
| 5 | Family | Visa-free passports only, with a search of W weeks vs a wider pool with visas handled | W, converted to money |
| 6 | Family | Pay on success vs monthly in advance plus a deposit | Fee reduction that makes B acceptable |
| 7 | Family | Pay yearly for the worker vs buy them out after year 1 | Buyout Y |
| 8 | Family | Relief guaranteed except July-August and holiday weeks vs all year | Fee difference (peak value) |
| 9 | Staff | Permanent job with one family vs employed rotation, paid between assignments | Pay in B (reservation wage) |
| 10 | Staff | Current non-travel role vs 6 months of rota work abroad | Extra pay; iterate at 3 and 9 months |

**Five-step study:**
1. Plan one sample: 12 family-side respondents (6 office or estate managers, 6 principals or PAs), 10 staff, and 5 agencies for qualitative interviews, all introduced through the channel in the base city.
2. Design the questionnaire from sections 5-6.
3. Pre-test with 2 respondents and redesign.
4. Interview interactively.
5. Process the answers with decision analysis.

**Outputs:** the distribution of V; P × Q at each fee, scaled to the potential market measured in T8 (not to 8,030 SFOs worldwide); segments by switching value and the bundling read; what goes in the base, as a premium, or out; the launch fee at MR = MC and the elasticity read; the USP (the difference with the largest V); and the staff reservation wage, which feeds A2.
