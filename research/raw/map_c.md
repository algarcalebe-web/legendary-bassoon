### D3(3) Incumbents and where they fail
- Luxury advisors/consortia (Virtuoso, Amex FHR/Centurion, Four Seasons-type agencies): strong on villa/suite supply; weak on cross-generation logistics (3-4 households, different passports, ages, mobility, dietary) and on education/governance content. Evidence: advisors themselves cite multigen as top growth (69%) but no tooling specific to it found.
- Education consultancies (IvyWise, Ivy Coach, Mandarin Oriental package): admissions-led, aviation bolted on; one-off. Not found: any player owning the whole annual calendar (summer, term transfers, tours, gap year).
- Boarding schools (Le Rosey etc.): include transport between own campuses only; term-start/exeat/guardian logistics left to families. (inference; no source on exeat pain found)
- Family-office retreat facilitators (KPMG, family-governance boutiques): content strong, destination/logistics generic; no pricing published.
- Teen programme operators (Rustic Pathways, Pacific Discovery): mass-affluent price points (US$4-9k), group model, little privacy/security for UHNW kids.
- Elder/medical: Medjet, Global Rescue (see alt_c.md) cover evacuation, not escorted ordinary travel.

### D3(4) 2023-2026 shifts
- Multigen is top advisor-reported trend (69%, 2025) and Virtuoso sales +~21% in 2026.
- Wealth transfer: 71% of family offices have not engaged next gen on purpose of wealth (Campden/AlTi 2025-26); creates demand for experiential governance/learning journeys.
- Education-as-luxury-travel is now productised at six figures (IvyWise US$300k).
- Privacy/safety: social-media exposure and K&R demand surge (uhnw_pains.md, 2026) apply doubly to children.

### D4(3) Incumbents and where they fail
- Placement agencies: Greycoat Lumleys (London; publishes salary survey; PA GBP 45-80k, butler GBP 45-60k, housekeeper GBP 40-65k in 2024-25 guideline), Polo & Tweed (Mayfair, CEO Lucy Challenger, butlers/nannies/yacht crew), Morgan & Mallet (regional offices US/UK/Europe/Dubai, in-house ATS), Eden Private Staff (NYC), Career Group. They fill permanent roles; the rota/travel layer (6-12 month rotations, temp cover, passport-matching, visas) sits at 2.36-4.82% of supply, i.e. agency databases are structurally short here.
- Fee model is contingent percentage on first-year salary (20-25% where published) with replacement guarantees, so incentive is placement not retention; tenure now ~3 years, so repeat fees recur but no agency owns the staff relationship.
- Vetting is agency-by-agency, jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction, no portable verified record. Evidence of failures is anecdotal (agency marketing).
- Yacht crew agencies handle STCW-certified chefs but not land/air rotations.
- Payroll/immigration for mobile staff: left to family office or immigration lawyers; no integrated player found.

### D4(4) 2023-2026 shifts
- Principals multi-hub (Dubai, US, Europe): staff must follow; Western-passport preference excludes capable non-Western staff (agency says so explicitly).
- Brexit effect on UK supply; post-2023 Dubai demand surge for travelling nannies, governesses.
- Pay at record highs; privacy premium 15-20%; tenure collapse to ~3 years raises turnover cost.
- Rising security threat (K&R demand, uhnw_pains.md) turns staff vetting into insurable risk.

### D9(3) Incumbents and where they fail
- Human PAs/EAs (US$80-250k; UK GBP 45-135k): high trust, but single point of failure, unbounded scope, 3-year tenure, pay inflation. Institutional memory of preferences leaves when they do.
- Family-office concierge desks: 47% of family offices offer no lifestyle services; 66% have 10 or fewer staff, so travel falls on the PA.
- Card concierges (Amex Platinum/Centurion, Capital One Concierge): scale, but request-fulfilment not planning; Amex is adding GenAI to consultants (Travel Counselor Assist).
- Luxury advisors/consortia (Virtuoso): trusted (96% say key) but human-hours-bound; only 12% use AI frequently; 85% prefer human.
- General AI agents (Operator, Google AI Mode, ChatGPT agent): good at commodity inventory (flights, OpenTable); lack access to UHNW supply (private villas, jets, off-market, privileged allocation), payment-identity and privacy controls. (inference from sources; no UHNW test found)

### D9(4) 2023-2026 shifts
- Agentic booking reached mainstream platforms (Operator Jan 2025, Google AI Mode Nov 2025+, Amex agentic strategy 2026).
- Advisor AI adoption doubling (frequent use 8% to 12%; non-users 24% to 9%).
- PA pay at record (up to US$190k+) while AI makes drafting/itinerary assembly cheap.
- Privacy premium (15-20%) and NDA norms mean AI tools handling UHNW data need on-prem/zero-retention positioning.

## Cross-domain observations
- The three domains share one hidden bottleneck: a single trusted human (PA/rota nanny/travelling chef) whose scarcity, pay inflation and 3-year tenure define the cost and fragility of UHNW travel.
- Biggest priced pools found: staff payroll (nanny/PA/chef US$70-300k each per year; yacht chef EUR 4-16k/month), boarding (US$138-145k/yr Le Rosey), education tours (US$25k-300k per trip), teen programmes (US$2-13.7k).
- Gaps in evidence: no UHNW-verified sample for multigen spend; no published fees for Greycoat Lumleys, Polo & Tweed, Bespoke Bureau, Imperial Domestic Staff; no UHNW delegation-rate statistic; no failure-rate data for vetting; US/UAE/Schengen staff-visa rules not researched; Capital One AI concierge details not found.
