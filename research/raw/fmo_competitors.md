## 4. Implications for the FMO wedge

1. The bundle itself is not occupied, but the compliance half is **less novel than the brief assumed**: M&M publishes multi-country EOR, insurance and visa support for household staff. A defensible FMO must lead with what M&M does not do: an owned rotating pool with relief cover and guaranteed replacement, passport and visa-routing diversity, and a portable record that outlives any one agency or family.
2. Supply is the moat, not software. M&M's own numbers (2.36% rota, 4.82% travel) say the scarce asset is staff willing and legally able to rotate. The employer-of-record that holds the staff contract and the visa relationship is the party that can capture them. M&M's remark that temp roles are hard to fill implies the pool must offer stable pay and benefits.
3. Likely paths for incumbents: M&M builds, Quay partners, TEAM/GTM resell. Plan for an acquirer or channel conversation with M&M, and for channel distribution through family-office networks (Simple's directory, TEAM's private-bank partners).
4. Trip medical for staff is already packaged by GTM with GeoBlue (US-centred); a cross-border pool needs its own insurer arrangement rather than a new insurance product.
5. Portable vetting: CrewPass proves willingness to pay (GBP 199 per check; GBP 9.99 per month) and is the template, but it is crew-only; nobody found offers an employer-agnostic record for nannies, chefs, drivers and housekeepers.
