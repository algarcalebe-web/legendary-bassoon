# Phase 3 (UHNW re-run): unmet needs, hypotheses and outside-in shortlist (2026-10-06)

Scope widened at the owner's request:
- **ultra-luxury and UHNW leisure travellers, globally**, not a corridor;
- ranked outside-in, with **moat width as a gate** (an option must score 3/5 or more on hard-to-copy);
- capital and founder capability are noted, not used to exclude.

Evidence: `raw/uhnw_pains.md`, `raw/uhnw_wtp.md`, `raw/uhnw_structure.md`, `raw/novelty_uhnw.md`, plus earlier phases. Most figures are search snippets; grades are in the raw files.

## 1. What changed vs the affluent analysis
- **The best-evidenced UHNW pains are trust, solvency, privacy and security, not planning time.**
  - Prepaid-deposit losses at private-aviation providers are documented in filed cases: JetSuite ~US$50M (2020); Verijet >US$10.5M; Jet It Ch. 7 (Dec 2025); OneFlight ~US$150M at risk (Sep 2026).
  - Illegal-charter fines run to US$1.5M and US$2.19M.
  - Jet tracking is enough of a problem that Arnault sold his jet.
  - High-end watch thefts rose from 12 a day (2021) to 21 a day (2024) (Watch Register).
- **UHNW travellers pay most for privacy, protection and someone to vouch for suppliers:**
  - private airport suites: PS US$4,850/yr + US$3,550 per visit; Heathrow Windsor from GBP 3,812 per visit;
  - close protection US$1,800-4,000/day;
  - broker markups of 10-20% on a ~US$16bn charter market;
  - yacht broker commission 15-20%.
- **They pay little for subscriptions:** K&R ~US$1-2k/yr; data removal US$39-400/yr.

## 5. Shortlist for Phases 4-5
1. **Counterparty assurance for private travel.** Stage 1 is U2, the standard and ratings, which is capital-light. Stage 2 is U1, insured deposit protection with an insurer partner. The ratings are the data that prices the protection, so the combination is harder to copy than either part. This is the only option where a two-sided network, a regulated wrapper and proprietary data stack up.
2. **Verified peer network** for UHNW travellers.
3. **Insurer-underwritten outcome guarantee.**

## 6. What could overturn this
- **Nobody may pay for assurance until after a loss.** No premium paid for deposit insurance was found. Willingness to pay is inferred from loss sizes and broker markups.
- **Incumbent extension.** ARGUS or Wyvern could add solvency checks, and Private Jet Card Comparisons could add financial vetting. Both are able; willingness is unknown.
- **Liability.** Publishing ratings of named firms invites legal action, so methodology and data access are critical.
- **The market is small in heads and large in stakes.** About 713,626 UHNW people (Knight Frank); thousands of jet-card holders; ~US$16bn charter market.
