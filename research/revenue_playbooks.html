Green Premium Pricing Kit: value calculators, CBAM pass-through model, WTP research kit, teardown club, async reviews and accelerator licences

B2B buyers already pay green premiums: Bain found more than 80% paid a premium on their last sustainable purchase, about a third will pay more than 5% now and 60% expect to within 3 years (SOURCED). But sellers struggle to justify the price, and EU rules now turn carbon into a cash line item: CBAM's definitive period started in 2026, with the first certificates bought from Feb 2027 (SOURCED). My searches found free Big-4 whitepapers (KPMG, Deloitte) and a member-only MIT toolkit, but no paid do-it-yourself green-premium pricing kit on Gumroad, Maven or the Professional Pricing Society store (SOURCED: search found none). Calebe's mix of pricing consulting, valuation, TCO modelling and engineering lets him build exactly this. Be honest about size: this niche is narrow. It is a high-margin satellite that clears maybe 15-30% of the US$10k floor in the base case, and its main value is feeding async-reviews, pro-firm-pricing and green-export-value.

Who buys

(1) Founders and commercial leads at seed-to-Series-B B2B cleantech and low-carbon product companies in the EU and US who must justify a price above the conventional product. (2) Pricing, commercial and sustainability managers at EU industrial firms selling low-carbon variants (steel, aluminium, cement, chemicals, packaging) and at EU importers exposed to CBAM. (3) Platform teams at climate accelerators and impact funds who need portfolio-support content. (4) Brazilian exporters to the EU hit by CBAM. Not consumers.

Then fold the assets elsewhere: rebrand GP-01 as a generic value/ROI calculator in model-templates and ai-roi-kit, move GP-02 into green-export-value, and post the teardowns as free yt-finance and LinkedIn content for pro-firm-pricing lead generation. Stop new building and keep the store live, passively. PIVOT at month 6 if digital sales are weak but reviews or licences sell: drop the course and teardown production, and sell only the US$690 review, the US$2,500 model build and licences (services-led). SCALE at month 6 if ≥4 reviews per month OR ≥2 licences OR email-to-paid ≥4%: raise the review to US$690 and the bundle to US$299; add a 4-week live cohort at US$990 per seat each quarter (max 20 seats); add a CBAM-specific mini-course for importers; hire a part-time VA (10 h/week, about US$400/month, ASSUMPTION) for data refresh and community posting; spend up to US$1k/month on retargeting only if 60-day payback is proven.

Risks

(1) Small niche: the green-premium pricing audience is far smaller than generic pricing or SaaS; the base case clears only about 30-50% of a US$10k floor by month 24. Mitigation: reuse the same engine in ai-roi-kit, model-templates and green-export-value. (2) Regulatory rollback lowers urgency: the CSRD Omnibus (provisional deal of 9 Dec 2025) raises scope to >1,000 employees and >EUR 450M turnover (SOURCED; final adoption not confirmed in my search). That shrinks compliance-driven demand, so lead with economics (TCO, CBAM cost, supplier switching), not reporting. CBAM's 50-tonne threshold exempts small importers (SOURCED). (3) US anti-ESG climate: US demand may need 'energy cost / TCO / efficiency' framing, not 'ESG'. (4) Claims liability: green-claims rules tighten. The EU Empowering Consumers Directive (2024/825) restricts generic environmental claims, applying from Sept 2026 (ASSUMPTION from memory; verify). Add disclaimers: educational, not legal or CBAM compliance advice; teardowns use only public data, and any premium not disclosed is marked UNKNOWN. (5) Calculator errors: CBAM free-allocation benchmark mechanics are complex. Publish a methodology note, version control, and a cap on liability in the terms. (6) Free competition: Big-4 and carbon-accounting SaaS publish free content and may add pricing modules. Differentiate on ready-to-use models plus human review. (7) Licensing sales cycles of 2-6 months and unknown budgets (accelerator licensing terms UNKNOWN). (8) Payout friction for a Brazilian seller (see brazil). (9) Content IP: Excel files get shared. Use PDF stamping and team licences, and sell updates and reviews, which cannot be pirated.
