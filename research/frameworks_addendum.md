# Framework addendum: what the five uploaded Deneffe decks add to the summary (2024 course decks)

The source decks are Strategy – Approach; The Strategy Process (abridged); Internal: Core Competence Analysis; Strategic Position Analysis; Markets with Network Effects. Only the frameworks are kept here. Course case examples are deliberately left out, per the brief.

| Framework element | What the decks add beyond `Deneffe_Frameworks_Summary.md` | How we apply it |
|---|---|---|
| **Profit measure** | Success is judged by **economic profit**: EVA = NOPAT − WACC × invested capital = (ROIC − WACC) × IC. It is not accounting profit. | Each option is judged on its WTP–cost wedge **and** the capital it ties up. Employing staff adds working capital (payroll before client payment, a relief bench). A capital-light design raises ROIC. |
| **Three analyses** | Strategy rests on external attractiveness, internal capabilities and **barriers to imitation** (rivals' ability and willingness to copy). | Imitation is analysed per rival, not just listed. |
| **"Job to be done" and monetization** | External analysis identifies the commercial potential of meeting a need ("the job to be done"), using the right monetization mechanism. | Each need is stated as a job (for example, "keep the household running across three countries without legal exposure"). The value metric is chosen to match it. |
| **Positioning** | Generic strategies (differentiation, cost leadership, "stuck in the middle") prove nothing. A position pays off depending on the distribution of preferences, the cost of serving them, rivals' positions, and the ability and willingness to imitate. Any position should be delivered at the lowest cost, and any cost position should carry maximum WTP. | The conclusion is the wedge, never a label. |
| **Grand Strategy outcomes** | Hypotheses on the most attractive options; an **ambition level**; **a priori boundaries** (businesses we will and will not consider); captured ideas. Vision, mission and values are statements of intent, not facts. | We state the ambition and the boundaries (for example: no owned aircraft, homes or inventory). Per the brief, no mission or vision statement. |
| **Limits of Grand Strategy** | A subjective basis; high/medium/low gut scores; little customer input; revenue forecasts on ad hoc assumptions rather than customer choice evidence; excessive belief in the vision; resistance to change. | Every forecast is labelled an assumption. Operational Strategy (trade-off interviews, choice evidence) validates, adapts or rejects. |
| **Core competence analysis: status** | Four statuses: I expired, II existing (current business), III under development (potential), IV needed in future (scenario-based). Classes: base, key, pacing. | Each competence gets class, position and status. |
| **Core competence: diagnostics** | Being a clear leader in a **base** competence is wasted investment. Less than favourable in a base competence is a fatal gap. Check for overinvestment in competences customers don't value. Base positions on evidence against rivals, not self-rating. | Base gaps are flagged as must-fix (partner or buy). Key and pacing competences are where differentiation must come from. |
| **SPA: criteria** | Tailor the criteria to the market. Attractiveness covers size, growth, competitive intensity, WTP, ease of doing business, risk and complexity. Position measures the ability to meet needs, or meet them at lower cost, than rivals. The more similar the segments, the more specific the criteria. | Scored sub-criteria per segment, not gut placement. |
| **SPA: display** | One slide. Bubble size = turnover or expected revenue. **Non-circular shapes for options not yet launched.** Medium-term horizon. Use in Grand Strategy, then firm up the numbers in a Phase 2 (operational) study. | New options are drawn as non-circular shapes, with bubbles sized on labelled expected revenue. |
| **Networks: types** | One-, two- or three-sided networks; same-side vs cross-side effects; positive or negative (congestion; same-side competition among sellers). | The staff pool is two-sided (families and staff): cross-side positive, same-side negative among families competing for scarce staff at peak. |
| **Networks: implications** | The objective is many high-value interactions, triggered by **marquee users**, before moving to adjacent geographies, segments or markets. Pricing below marginal cost, or permanent subsidy of one side, can be rational. Traditional barriers may not hold. **Envelopment** by a platform with an overlapping user base that bundles the function. | Win marquee family offices first. Consider subsidising the staff side (training, insurance, benefits). Watch envelopment by agencies or EOR platforms. |
| **Winner-take-all** | All three conditions must hold. Condition 1 (high multi-homing cost for at least one side) is sufficient but not necessary. | Applied unchanged. |
