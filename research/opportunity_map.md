# Opportunity map: the UHNW life around travel (2026-10-06)

**What this round did.** The owner asked to drop the anchoring claim and the assurance-first framing and go wider. This map covers 10 domains of UHNW life around travel and merges 20 new seeds from `raw/map_a.md` to `raw/map_d.md` with the earlier hypotheses.

**How it is scored.** All scores are outside-in (our assessment, 1-5 each), out of 25:
- **Pain**: severity x frequency for UHNW families.
- **Gap**: how poorly the best alternative performs.
- **WTP**: revealed willingness to pay, i.e. evidence of money already spent on the problem.
- **Moat**: the gate. An idea needs 3 or more to stay in.
- **Pool**: size of the spend pool.

Founder fit is not scored. Capital is a flag, not a filter: L, M or H.

**Evidence caveat.** Almost all figures are search summaries, and most samples are affluent or industry-wide, not verified US$30M+ households. Novelty was checked only at seed level. The final pick needs a full novelty check.

## 3. Themes and a platform thesis

| Theme | Members (score) | Who pays | Why it could be wide-moat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. People who move with the family** | Staff and crew pool (19); staff compliance + passport (18); crew bench (16) | Families, family offices, yacht owners (salaries GBP 55k-US$300k; agency fees 12-25%) | Exclusive supply of the scarce 2-5% who can rotate; compliance data and licences; switching costs once staff are on our payroll |
| **B. Records that move with the family** | Residency ledger (18); health continuity (19); portable identity (15) | Families via tax advisers, physicians, family offices | Data that compounds; switching costs; licences (medical, adviser sign-off) |
| **C. Trust in suppliers and money** | Counterparty assurance (19); event escrow (16); anti-impersonation (16) | Family offices, banks, buyers of jets, yachts and events | Standard-setting, loss data, insurer partners |
| D. Homes and assets | Multi-home pool (17); berth exchange (16); fractional governance (15) | Owners | Exclusive supply (berths, staff), switching costs |
| E. Reputation and purpose | SAF registry (16); next-gen retreats (13) | Public-profile flyers, family offices | Accreditation, data |

**Platform thesis (hypothesis, not a scored option).** A principal-owned **family mobility office** could combine themes A and B, with C as a module:
- vetted staff who rotate with the family;
- their visas and payroll;
- the family's residency days;
- health records;
- identity credentials;
- supplier trust.

It would sell to family offices, which pay most and are served worst (UBS: ~47% offer no lifestyle services; Campden: low satisfaction).

The moat would compound across modules: one data vault, one network of vetted people, rising switching costs. The risk is that it is too broad to start. It only works if entered through **one wedge** that earns cash and trust first. Its total would be inferred from the wedges, so it is not ranked alongside them.

## 4. What changed vs the previous round
- Counterparty assurance still scores 19, but it no longer stands alone. **Two staff ideas and one health idea tie or nearly tie it, with stronger evidence of willingness to pay** (salaries and agency fees; retreat and membership prices are observed, not inferred).
- The strongest evidence of money already spent sits in **people (staff) and health**. The strongest moats sit in **data that moves with the family**.
- The original curator concept scores 11 and fails the moat gate.
